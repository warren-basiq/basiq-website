# VP of Revenue Operations Persona Profile (Basiq Platform)

## Basic Profile

**Title:** VP of Revenue Operations, Director of Revenue Operations
**Title Variations:** Head of RevOps, Senior Director of GTM Operations, VP Sales Operations, Head of Growth Operations
**Company Size:** 100–5,000 employees, B2B SaaS or tech-enabled services with multi-meeting sales cycles, an outbound motion, and a CS function
**Team Size / Scope:** 2–15 (ops analysts, marketing ops, sometimes a GTM engineer); owns systems, data, and process across sales, marketing, and CS
**Industry:** B2B SaaS, fintech, tech-enabled services
**Reports to:** CRO, COO, or CEO
**Direct Reports:** RevOps analysts, GTM engineers, marketing ops
**Budget Responsibility:** GTM tech stack budget, typically $500K–$2M+ annually; final call on most tool adoption. One of the three signers in the Basiq buying committee, alongside CRO and VP Sales.

---

## Context

RevOps is the person who designed every process the revenue org runs and controls the execution of none of them. They own the data, and the data depends on reps typing. They own the playbook, and the playbook has ~30% adoption. They own the AI mandate, and the context feeding the AI goes stale on its own. Every tool they have ever rolled out was experienced by sellers as a reporting tax, which is precisely why the data underneath it all is incomplete.

The Basiq pitch to this persona is the mechanism of the end state. The future of GTM is a seller working a served queue: every task in priority order, each generated from the full context of every meeting. RevOps buys that future because of what falls out of it. Complete data is the exhaust of the queue, verified execution is readable from task completion, and the org's canonical context updates itself with RevOps holding the approval gate. Where the CEO buys the vision and the CRO buys the outcomes, RevOps buys and operates the flywheel itself.

Entry-point discipline still applies: this persona might arrive through the data-and-process pain family or the AI-operationalization pain family. The persona doc unifies; campaigns stay separate.

## Goals & Success Metrics

### Primary Goals

1. **Make the full revenue funnel predictable.** Pipeline generation, conversion, and retention data all credible enough to plan against.
2. **Get data quality without enforcement.** CRM and reporting accuracy that does not depend on reps and CSMs doing homework they will never do.
3. **Make process compliance verifiable.** Qualification frameworks, stage gates, and playbook execution that provably happened, per rep, per play, across sales and CS.
4. **Own AI operationalization for the GTM org.** Leadership declared AI mission-critical and the mandate landed on RevOps; making it demonstrably work is now core to the role.
5. **Keep the org's canonical context current.** Personas, value props, and competitive intel that reflect what buyers said this month, not what the offsite produced last year.
6. **Make the handoffs work.** Lead-to-deal and sale-to-CS transitions that transfer context reliably instead of depending on templates nobody fills out.
7. **Consolidate the stack and defend every line item.** Fewer tools, clearer ROI, AI spend traceable to plays and outcomes.
8. **Grow into the CRO-track operator role.** RevOps leaders who can demonstrably run an AI-era, full-funnel revenue machine are the ones moving up.

### Key Performance Indicators

- Pipeline generated and pipeline per dollar of GTM spend
- CRM data quality and completeness across sales and CS objects
- Playbook execution rates, verifiable per rep and per play
- Forecast accuracy for new business and renewals
- Rep and CSM time spent selling versus admin
- Reply rate, meeting conversion, and cost per qualified meeting on outbound
- Stack cost, tool utilization, and AI spend per outcome
- Time-to-current for context docs after a positioning or market change

## Major Hurdles to Achieving Goals

### Hurdle 1: **Data quality depends on self-reporting, and every fix has been a reporting tax**

**The Problem:** Reps do not update deals accurately and CSMs do not log account reality. Fields are incomplete, stale, or aspirational, from first touch through renewal. Every remedy RevOps has deployed, mandatory fields, dashboards, commission gates, was built for leadership and tolerated by sellers, so compliance improves for six weeks and drifts back. One study found 24% of CRM admins report less than half their data is accurate and complete.

**The Impact:** Leadership cannot trust the forecast in either book, reports mislead, and RevOps spends its hours as data janitors instead of strategic operators. Worse, RevOps gets identified as the function that makes sellers do paperwork.

**What They've Tried:** Mandatory fields, training, dashboards shaming incomplete records. Behavior-change approaches fail because data entry benefits everyone except the person doing it.

---

### Hurdle 2: **Process compliance is unverifiable, so the playbook is a hope**

**The Problem:** MEDDIC frameworks, stage gates, QBR cadences, and handoff checklists exist on paper. Average playbook adoption sits around 30%, and reps follow the designed process about 36% of the time, because the playbook is a doc they have to visit while the decision is happening in the deal. Whether qualification actually happened is unknowable without interrogating people.

**The Impact:** Unqualified deals pollute the pipeline, at-risk accounts hide behind completed checklists, and when the number misses, RevOps cannot tell leadership whether the process failed or was never run. The gap makes RevOps look like bureaucracy rather than enablement.

**What They've Tried:** Enablement sessions, checkbox fields, manager reinforcement. Compliance theater, performed in CRM fields.

---

### Hurdle 3: **The org's canonical context goes stale on its own, and every tool has its own aging copy**

**The Problem:** The value props and personas feeding outbound, deal analysis, and expansion recommendations live in a deck from two quarters ago, pasted as snapshots into Clay columns, sequencer templates, and battlecards, each copy aging differently. Sales and CS calls surface new objections and shifting buyer language daily, and none of it flows back. RevOps's own peers name lack of context for the AI as a top blocker to AI working in GTM.

**The Impact:** Output quality degrades quietly everywhere at once, prospects get inconsistent messaging depending on which play touched them, and the AI writes to a buyer that no longer exists. Reply rates drift down with no identifiable cause.

**What They've Tried:** Quarterly persona refresh projects. A new deck gets produced, and the automation keeps running on whatever was pasted in before the offsite.

---

### Hurdle 4: **No feedback loop from outcomes back to the assets**

**The Problem:** RevOps can edit every prompt and every context doc in the stack. What they cannot do is know what to edit. Replies, meeting outcomes, and deal results never attach back to the specific prompts and context versions that produced them. When performance dips, the diagnosis is guesswork across copy, list, persona, model, and domain health.

**The Impact:** Iteration runs on instinct across dozens of plays. Industry reply rates fell from 8.5% to 3.43% while elite senders exceed 10%; the gap is feedback discipline, and RevOps has no mechanism for it.

---

### Hurdle 5: **Handoffs are process documents, not systems**

**The Problem:** RevOps designed the lead routing, the sales-to-CS handoff template, and the transition call checklist. Adoption is inconsistent, the information that transfers degrades under quota pressure, and RevOps gets blamed when the seams fail because the seams are "their process."

**The Impact:** Onboarding starts cold, deal context evaporates at stage transitions, and RevOps defends a process failure they had no mechanical way to prevent.

---

### Hurdle 6: **The stack sprawls and its AI spend is untraceable**

**The Problem:** Eight to twelve tools across the funnel that integrate poorly, plus AI line items, Clay credits, LLM API costs, AI features inside every vendor, scattered with no mapping to plays or outcomes. Nobody knows which model each task should use from a cost and performance standpoint, so models drift and output quality degrades silently.

**The Impact:** Budget defense gets harder every cycle, efficient and wasteful spend get funded identically, and the consolidation discipline RevOps applies everywhere else cannot reach the AI layer.

## How Basiq Aligns With Overcoming These Hurdles

### Basiq Solution to Hurdle 1: Complete data as the exhaust of the served day

**How Basiq Helps:** Basiq serves every seller their exact next task in priority order, generated from the full context of their conversation history. As reps work the queue, everything is captured: CRM records, stakeholder maps, stages, and next steps fan out from the meeting transcript. The flywheel runs itself: context produces the right tasks, right tasks get done, done tasks produce complete data, complete data sharpens the next tasks.

**Specific Benefits:**
- Data completeness stops being a discipline program; nobody has to remember to enter anything
- Adoption is structural, not mandated: the seller surface leads, so reps use it because it makes their day easier
- Data quality holds across pre-sale and post-sale because the queue spans AEs, AMs, and CSMs
- RevOps hours move from cleanup to analysis
- Leadership reporting stops requiring caveats

**The Outcome:** Clean, trustworthy funnel data without an enforcement regime, for the first time in this person's career.

---

### Basiq Solution to Hurdle 2: Execution verified from served tasks, not checkboxes

**How Basiq Helps:** Because the playbook reaches sellers as served tasks and completion is tracked, "was the process run" becomes readable data. Deals get scored against qualification frameworks from what was actually said, per rep, per stage, per play.

**Specific Benefits:**
- Gap detection shows exactly which qualification elements are missing per deal
- Execution visible per rep and per play, so coaching targets the specific step being skipped
- When results miss on verified execution, RevOps can tell leadership whether the problem is sellers, message, or plays
- The process police role disappears because the system carries the process

**The Outcome:** Real process adoption with verification built in, and RevOps becomes the function that can answer the question everyone else guesses at.

---

### Basiq Solution to Hurdle 3: The approve-or-decline loop, with RevOps holding the gate

**How Basiq Helps:** After every call, Basiq compares the transcript against the persona and value proposition docs and proposes specific changes. RevOps or enablement approves or declines each one, and the approved version is what every prompt in the platform reads from on the next run. Musal serves the canonical, versioned assets to every consumer.

**Specific Benefits:**
- The snapshot-pasting pattern ends; every play references the current version
- RevOps becomes the editor of the org's canonical context, an auditable loop with a human gate
- Context reflects current objections and buyer language, updated from the field after every meeting
- One approved change propagates to every play, rep, and stage at once
- Fix the asset base once and quality improves at every stage simultaneously

**The Outcome:** The knowledge behind the machine stays as current as the machine, and RevOps runs the loop instead of the refresh project.

---

### Basiq Solution to Hurdle 4: The loop, closed

**How Basiq Helps:** Outputs and outcomes attach back to the specific prompt and context versions that produced them, so iteration runs on evidence instead of instinct.

**Specific Benefits:**
- Per-prompt performance visible and attributable
- Underperforming plays surface with candidate improvements attached
- Version history connects performance movement to specific changes
- Diagnosis separates copy, context, and model instead of guessing

**The Outcome:** The gap between average and elite execution becomes a workflow RevOps runs.

---

### Basiq Solution to Hurdle 5: Handoffs as data flow, not compliance ask

**How Basiq Helps:** The queue spans pre-sale and post-sale in one system, so the handoffs are internal to the platform. Prospecting relationship history carries into the deal; deal context including champion, economic buyer, objections, and commitments carries into the account the CSM inherits. Nothing depends on someone filling out a template.

**Specific Benefits:**
- Handoff quality becomes inspectable data instead of an honor system
- Onboarding starts from full context by default
- RevOps stops defending process failures they could not mechanically prevent
- Handoff completeness is reportable, so accountability lands where it belongs

**The Outcome:** The seams RevOps designed on paper finally exist in the system.

---

### Basiq Solution to Hurdle 6: Model choice and AI spend with receipts

**How Basiq Helps:** Musal gives visibility and control over model choice per task, with cost and performance in view. It does not route automatically; RevOps sees and controls the decision. Consolidation replaces the SEP, deal intelligence, and customer management layers with one platform and one data spine.

**Specific Benefits:**
- AI spend mapped to the plays and outcomes it powers
- Model drift stops degrading output quality silently
- Fewer tools, fewer integration seams, fewer vendor negotiations
- Per-stage ROI measurable in one system

**The Outcome:** Stack ROI discipline finally extends to the whole funnel and the AI layer.

## Why This Persona Cares About Basiq

RevOps sits at the seam where the org's AI ambitions and process ambitions both meet operational reality. Leadership handed them AI operationalization without the mechanisms to run it; sales and CS leaders want insight from data those same teams will not maintain; the CRO wants full-funnel efficiency numbers the fragmented stack cannot produce. Both blockers their own industry names, unclean data and lack of context for the AI, land on this one desk.

**The core dilemma: how do they run a predictable, AI-powered revenue machine when the data underneath it depends on self-reporting, the playbook they wrote runs 30% of the time, the knowledge feeding the AI goes stale on its own, and nothing tells them what is working?** They can edit everything and verify nothing. They own every process and control none of its execution.

**The emotional trigger** is accountability without instrumentation. Every quarter of drifting reply rates, dirty data, and broken handoffs erodes their standing in the rooms where a CRO-track reputation gets built, and the honest explanation sounds like an excuse without a system behind it.

**The buying motivation** is operating the flywheel. Basiq gives them data captured as the exhaust of sellers working a queue they actually want to use, execution verified from served tasks, canonical context that updates itself with RevOps holding the approval gate, handoffs that run as data flows, and AI spend they can defend per play. It converts RevOps from the function that reports on the revenue org to the function that runs its execution layer, which is the strongest version of the case for their next role.

## Differentiation Notes (vs. adjacent Basiq personas)

**vs. CRO:** RevOps buys and operates the mechanism; the CRO buys the outcomes. RevOps messaging leads with instrumentation, data capture, verification, and the loop. CRO messaging leads with predictable streams, closed handoffs, and board credibility. Same platform, two altitudes; RevOps is typically the deployer and often the economic buyer, with the CRO as sponsor.

**vs. Founder/CEO:** The CEO buys the vision (the future of GTM) and the de-risked capital bet; verification is their payoff and someone else runs the system. RevOps is that someone. Never send CEO-altitude vision framing to RevOps without the mechanism underneath it; RevOps's first question is "how does the capture actually work."

**vs. GTM Engineer:** The critical pairing. The GTM Engineer builds the plays; RevOps owns the machine, its data, its cost, and its outcomes. The engineer's pains are craft-level and daily; RevOps's are system-level and quarterly. The engineer discovers Basiq and champions it; RevOps signs. Never send governance messaging to the builder or craft-workflow messaging to the owner.

**Use this persona for:** outbound to VP/Director RevOps at 100–5,000 person B2B companies with multi-meeting cycles, an outbound motion, and a CS function; content on data quality without enforcement, execution verification, the approve-or-decline context loop, GTM AI governance, and stack consolidation; sales motions where RevOps is the economic buyer.

**Do not use this persona for:** hands-on Clay workflow content (GTM Engineer), executive vision or board-narrative content (CEO, CRO), or single-product deal-execution campaigns.
