# VP of Revenue Operations Sequence Brief (Basiq Platform)

**Who:** VP or Director of Revenue Operations, 100–5,000 employees, B2B with multi-meeting cycles, an outbound motion, and a CS function. Often the economic buyer; always the deployer.
**Reserved word: Control.** This card is about controlling the machine: capture, verification, the context gate, spend. Not proof of the motion (CEO's frame), not predictability of the number (CRO's frame), not quota (VP Sales' frame). If a message drifts toward board narrative or team performance, it's on the wrong card.

---

## Triggers

### Role and team signals

**New VP/Director RevOps in seat within the last 120 days.**
New operators audit the stack and the data in their first quarter, and arrive with a mandate to fix what the last regime enforced. Best window this persona ever has.
→ *Pain 1 (self-reported data), Pain 6 (untraceable spend)*

**RevOps analyst or GTM engineer job postings.**
They're adding headcount to clean data and build plays. Capacity added to a capture problem is capacity spent on janitorial work.
→ *Pain 1, Pain 4 (no feedback loop)*

**Sales enablement hire or "sales process" language in job postings.**
Someone is being hired to drive playbook adoption. The playbook runs ~30% of the time today, and the new hire inherits the same delivery mechanism that produced that number.
→ *Pain 2 (unverifiable compliance)*

### Stack and stated concerns

**RevOps leader posts on LinkedIn about data quality, AI in GTM, tool sprawl, or rep adoption.**
Strongest personalization available. This persona posts about their pains more than any other in the committee; quote their framing back and extend it.
→ *Pain 1, 3, or 6 depending on the post*

**Company announces an AI initiative or AI-first mandate.**
The mandate lands on RevOps without the mechanisms. Their own industry names unclean data and missing AI context as the top two blockers.
→ *Pain 3 (stale context), Pain 1*

**Stack signals: Gong + Clay + a sequencer visible in job posts or tech-stack data.**
Four tools, four data silos, four aging copies of the personas. The assembled stack is the competitor, and its seams are this persona's daily life.
→ *Pain 3, Pain 5 (handoffs), Pain 6*

### Org events

**New CRO hired.**
The new CRO asks for numbers the fragmented stack can't produce, and RevOps has one quarter to show instrumentation. Stack reviews follow new CROs like weather.
→ *Pain 2, Pain 6*

**Repositioning, rebrand, or new product launch.**
New messaging has to propagate into every Clay column, sequence, and battlecard. Today that's a manual paste job that will be half-done in a month.
→ *Pain 3*

**Efficiency push visible in layoffs, hiring freezes, or consolidation language.**
Every line item is about to be defended. AI spend with no mapping to outcomes is the hardest line to defend.
→ *Pain 6*

**Sales kickoff season (Dec–Feb).**
A new playbook is about to launch into the same delivery mechanism that gave the last one 30% adoption. Reaching them pre-launch beats post-mortem.
→ *Pain 2*

## Pain Points

**The wedge. If a message lands only one, land this one.**

### 1. Data quality depends on self-reporting, and every fix has been a reporting tax

Reps don't update deals, CSMs don't log account reality, and every remedy RevOps ever deployed was built for leadership and resented by sellers. Compliance improves for six weeks and drifts back. RevOps became the paperwork function.
**Say it as:** "Your data quality problem is a capture problem. Stop asking reps to be scribes."
**Numbers:** 24% of CRM admins report less than half their data is accurate and complete (Validity via Databar).

### 2. You wrote the playbook and can't prove it ran

Qualification frameworks and stage gates exist on paper. Whether they happened is unknowable without interrogating people, so compliance is theater performed in CRM fields, and when the number misses RevOps can't tell leadership whether the process failed or was never run.
**Say it as:** "You own every process and control none of its execution."
**Numbers:** ~30% playbook adoption (Chief); reps follow the designed process ~36% of the time (State of Sales Enablement); embedded-process reps hit quota at 3.7x (Gartner CSO via Supered).

### 3. Every tool has its own aging copy of your personas

The value props feeding outbound, deal analysis, and expansion recs are snapshots pasted into Clay columns, sequences, and battlecards two quarters ago, each aging differently. Buyer language shifts daily and none of it flows back.
**Say it as:** "The machine is current. Its knowledge isn't."
**Numbers:** RevOps leaders name unclean data and lack of AI context as the top two blockers to AI in GTM (Default, State of AI in RevOps H1 2026).

### 4. You can edit everything and diagnose nothing

Full access to every prompt and context doc, zero signal about which ones work. Outcomes never attach back to the versions that produced them, so when performance dips the diagnosis is guesswork across copy, list, persona, and model.
**Say it as:** "Full access to every prompt, zero signal about any of them."
**Numbers:** Reply rates fell from 8.5% (2019) to 3.43% (2026) while elite senders exceed 10% (Instantly). The gap is feedback discipline.

### 5. The handoffs are your process, so the failures are your fault

Lead routing, the sales-to-CS template, the transition checklist: RevOps designed all of it, controls none of it, and gets blamed when context evaporates at the seams.
**Say it as:** "You designed the handoff. You get blamed for it. You never had a mechanical way to make it happen."

### 6. The AI spend is untraceable and the models drift

Clay credits, LLM API costs, AI features inside every vendor, none mapped to plays or outcomes. Nobody knows which model each task should use, so quality degrades silently and budget defense gets harder every cycle.
**Say it as:** "Efficient spend and wasteful spend are getting funded identically, because nothing tells you which is which."
**Use with care:** implies they've lost control of their own budget. Works as an industry observation, not a diagnosis of them.

## Proof Points

RevOps's doubt is mechanical: "how does the capture actually work, and why would reps use this when they've resented everything I've rolled out?" The sequence answers those two questions in order. Traction is never volunteered.

**1. The live capture mechanism.** A real transcript goes in; CRM fields, stakeholder maps, tasks, and proposed doc edits come out, watchable in minutes. Leads because this persona's first question is always how the capture works, and the demo is the answer no deck can give.

**2. The adoption design argument.** Every mandate-based tool produced incomplete data, and this persona has the scar tissue to prove it. Basiq's seller surface leads: reps work the queue because it makes their day easier, and completeness is the exhaust. Second because it answers the objection their whole career has taught them: "reps won't use it."

**3. The approval gate, shown live.** After a call, proposed edits to the persona and value prop docs land in a queue this persona controls. Approve or decline; the approved version feeds every play at once. Third because it's the role upgrade: from process police to editor of the org's canonical context. Research found no other product doing this loop, so "nobody else does this" is usable here.

**4. The architecture honesty.** Salesforce or HubSpot stays as the system of record; Basiq changes what feeds it. Fourth and higher than on the CEO card, because this persona owns the CRM and a migration project is an automatic no.

**5. The structural research.** 30% adoption, 36% process-following, 24% CRM data admission, top-two AI blockers. Fifth because RevOps already knows these numbers; they confirm rather than persuade. Use to build peer credibility, not to open.

**6. Operator credibility.** Built by founders who run revenue motions for client organizations. Hold for "who are you people."

**Sequencing:** capture mechanism first, adoption design second, approval gate third. Architecture, research, and credibility are held responses.

**The honest gap:** nothing yet proves outcomes at scale. No deployment across a 50-rep org, no measured data-quality improvement, no reference customer this persona can call. Additionally: do not demo-promise per-prompt outcome attribution (Pain 4's full solution) beyond what's actually shipped; the approve-loop and model-visibility claims are safe, the closed attribution loop needs a product check before it's claimed live. If asked about traction, say early and plainly. This persona diligences vendors for a living and will find out anyway.
