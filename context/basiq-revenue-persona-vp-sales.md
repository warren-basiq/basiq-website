# VP of Sales Persona Profile (Basiq Platform)

## Basic Profile

**Title:** VP of Sales, SVP of Sales, Head of Sales
**Title Variations:** VP of Revenue (where sales-only scope), Director of Sales at the smaller end
**Company Size:** 100–5,000 employees, B2B SaaS and technology with multi-meeting sales cycles
**Team Size / Scope:** 10–150 quota-carrying reps (AEs, SDRs) plus frontline sales managers; owns the new-business number
**Industry:** B2B SaaS, technology, tech-enabled services
**Reports to:** CRO or CEO
**Direct Reports:** Sales directors, frontline managers, AEs
**Budget Responsibility:** Sales headcount plan and sales-specific tooling; one of the three signers in the Basiq buying committee, alongside CRO and VP RevOps. Signs where sales tooling is their line; co-signs where the CRO owns the platform decision.

---

## Context

The VP of Sales owns the most exposed number in the company: new business, this quarter, with the team they have. Everyone above them holds a longer clock. The CEO thinks in funding cycles, the CRO in annual plans. The VP Sales gets graded every 90 days, and average tenure in the seat has fallen to 17–19 months, often shorter than one enterprise sales cycle. Every quarter is an audition.

Their core operating problem is dispersion. The top three reps carry the number, the middle sixty percent underperforms a playbook that only runs about a third of the time, and the VP can't see execution at the rep level until it shows up in the miss. They manage through frontline managers who have no bandwidth to coach, pipeline reviews built on data reps typed under duress, and a forecast that is rep confidence compounded upward.

The Basiq pitch to this persona is the team's day, transformed. Every rep works a served queue: prep, post-game, follow-ups, prospecting, deal progression, each task generated from the full context of every meeting. The playbook stops being a doc the middle of the team ignores and becomes the default path through the day. The VP buys attainment spread across the whole team, and the visibility to coach the specific step being skipped.

## Goals & Success Metrics

### Primary Goals

1. **Hit the number this quarter, and make next quarter's number visible now.** Pipeline coverage and conversion both, with no end-of-quarter scramble.
2. **Spread attainment beyond the top three reps.** The team's number can't depend on whether the A-players have a good quarter.
3. **Protect and expand selling time.** Reps spend 60% of their time not selling; the VP is paying for a team at half its effective capacity.
4. **Make coaching change behavior at scale.** Frontline managers coach the specific gaps that lose deals, not whatever last week's escalation surfaced.
5. **Ramp new reps inside two quarters.** At 5–6 months average AE ramp and roughly 27% of new B2B reps never hitting quota, every hire is a bet the VP needs to de-risk.
6. **Call the quarter accurately.** A forecast the CRO and CEO can take upward without a haircut.
7. **Earn the CRO seat.** VPs who can show a system that produces the number, not just a quarter that happened to land, are the ones who move up.

### Key Performance Indicators

- Quota attainment, aggregate and by rep
- Pipeline coverage ratio and pipeline created per rep
- Win rate and stage-by-stage conversion
- Sales cycle length
- Forecast accuracy (committed vs. closed)
- New rep ramp time and cohort attainment
- Selling time per rep vs. admin time
- Rep retention, especially in the middle of the performance curve

## Major Hurdles to Achieving Goals

### Hurdle 1: **Attainment is top-heavy, and the middle of the team can't be fixed one rep at a time**

**The Problem:** Average B2B quota attainment sits around 47%, and only 15% of sales teams have more than half their reps hitting even 80% of quota. The VP's number is carried by the top performers while the middle sixty percent runs their own improvised version of the sales process. Reps follow the designed process about 36% of the time, and what the top reps do differently is invisible, so it can't be transferred.

**The Impact:** The number depends on a handful of people, which means one departure or one bad quarter from an A-player breaks the plan. Reps working with embedded process systems hit quota at 3.7x the rate of reps relying on memory; that entire gap is sitting inside the VP's team right now, unaddressed.

**What They've Tried:** Methodology rollouts, enablement sessions, call libraries, pairing average reps with top performers. Each moves the needle for six weeks, then the middle reverts to improvisation.

---

### Hurdle 2: **The team sells at half its headcount because non-selling work eats the day**

**The Problem:** Reps spend roughly 60% of their time on non-selling tasks: pre-call research they'll mostly forget, CRM updates that are stale on arrival, internal meetings, and figuring out what to do next across five tools. Gartner finds half of sellers overwhelmed by the number of platforms they're required to use.

**The Impact:** A 20-rep team performs like a 10-rep team, and the VP gets asked to hit a bigger number with it anyway. Activity reports look fine while actual selling conversations stay flat. The AE self-sourcing mandate becomes an unfunded tax on the same shrinking selling window.

**What They've Tried:** Sequencers, note-takers, more SDR support. Each tool automates a slice and adds its own overhead, and the rep's day is still self-navigated.

---

### Hurdle 3: **Coaching doesn't scale, so it goes to whoever is already on fire**

**The Problem:** Meaningful coaching takes about three hours per rep per month, and frontline managers spend 30–60% of their time on admin and meetings. Only 40% of reps get weekly coaching, the frequency that reliably moves attainment. Call recordings exist, but reviewing them is a job nobody has time for, so coaching is reactive: the deal that just died, the rep already struggling.

**The Impact:** The middle of the performance curve, exactly where coaching pays best, gets almost nothing. The gap between top and average widens every quarter, and 75% of reps say they'd be more likely to hit target with real coaching they aren't getting.

**What They've Tried:** Conversation intelligence tools that record everything and change little, coaching cadences that collapse under quota pressure, film review sessions that reach three reps a month.

---

### Hurdle 4: **Pipeline reviews are interrogations because the data is fiction**

**The Problem:** Deal state is whatever reps typed in, whenever they got around to it. Stages stale, close dates rolling 30 days on repeat, notes that read "good call." One study found 24% of CRM admins report less than half their data is accurate and complete. So the VP runs pipeline reviews as cross-examinations, extracting deal reality verbally, one deal at a time.

**The Impact:** Reviews burn hours and produce a forecast that is rep confidence compounded upward. Roughly 44% of deals slip, at-risk deals hide until they die, and the VP hands the CRO a commit they privately discount.

**What They've Tried:** Mandatory fields, forecast categories, deal inspection cadences. Compliance theater; the underlying data stays self-reported.

---

### Hurdle 5: **Ramp takes half a year and a quarter of hires never get there**

**The Problem:** Average AE ramp runs 5–6 months, longer above $50K ACV, and about 27% of new B2B reps never hit quota at all. The new rep inherits a 47-page playbook that's already stale, accounts with no usable history, and a motion they have to reconstruct by shadowing whoever has time.

**The Impact:** Every hire is two quarters of cost before production, on the VP's 17–19 month clock. A slow-ramping cohort quietly kills the annual plan months before it shows in the number, and the VP can't tell a bad hire from a bad handoff.

**What They've Tried:** Onboarding bootcamps, certifications, shadowing programs. Ramp across B2B SaaS has gotten longer over five years anyway, because products got more complex while onboarding got leaner.

---

### Hurdle 6: **Deals die from skipped steps, not lost arguments**

**The Problem:** The follow-up that never went out, the champion who never got multi-threaded, the stakeholder nobody mapped, the recap that would have kept momentum. The VP can see all of it in the post-mortem and none of it in time. Execution discipline is enforced by rep memory and motivation, which is to say it isn't.

**The Impact:** Winnable pipeline converts below its potential, cycle length stretches as deals stall in silence, and the loss reasons in the CRM say "price" and "timing" when the honest answer is "we stopped working it."

**What They've Tried:** Sequences, mutual action plans, manager check-ins on next steps. All of it depends on the rep remembering to run it.

## How Basiq Aligns With Overcoming These Hurdles

### Basiq Solution to Hurdle 1: The playbook becomes the default path through the day

**How Basiq Helps:** Basiq serves every rep their exact next tasks in priority order: prep, post-game, multi-threading, follow-ups, prospecting, each generated from the full context of every meeting. The designed motion stops being a doc the middle of the team ignores and becomes the easiest way to work.

**Specific Benefits:**
- The middle sixty percent runs the same motion as the top performers, because the motion is the queue
- Adoption is structural: reps work the queue because it makes their day easier, not because it was mandated
- Execution is visible per rep, per stage, per play, so dispersion has an address
- The 3.7x embedded-process gap gets captured instead of paid for

**The Outcome:** Attainment spreads across the team, and the number stops depending on three people.

---

### Basiq Solution to Hurdle 2: Selling capacity recovered without headcount

**How Basiq Helps:** Non-selling work is automated away, not merely tracked. Prep arrives generated, recaps draft themselves, CRM updates fan out from the transcript, and the rep never reconstructs context before acting.

**Specific Benefits:**
- Hours per rep per week return to actual selling conversations
- Pre-call research stops being a 30-minute ritual; the context is already behind the task
- The self-sourcing mandate becomes a served motion with prospecting tasks in the same queue
- Effective team capacity rises against the same headcount line

**The Outcome:** The VP hits a bigger number with the team they already have.

---

### Basiq Solution to Hurdle 3: Coaching aimed at the specific step being skipped

**How Basiq Helps:** Because tasks are served and completion is tracked, the VP and frontline managers see exactly which plays each rep runs and skips. Post-game output shows MEDDIC movement per deal, so coaching conversations start from evidence, not recollection.

**Specific Benefits:**
- Coaching targets the specific skipped step, per rep, instead of generic film review
- The middle of the curve finally gets structured attention, because finding the gap no longer costs manager hours
- Frontline managers coach with the evidence pre-assembled
- What top performers do differently becomes visible and teachable

**The Outcome:** Coaching frequency and precision both rise without adding manager bandwidth.

---

### Basiq Solution to Hurdle 4: Pipeline review on evidence, not interrogation

**How Basiq Helps:** The meeting transcript is the source of truth. Stages, next steps, stakeholder maps, and forecast inputs fan out from what actually happened in conversations, and data completeness is the exhaust of reps working the queue. Salesforce or HubSpot stays; what changes is what feeds it.

**Specific Benefits:**
- Deal state is something the system knows, not something reps report
- Zombie deals surface when qualification gaps stay unfilled
- Review time goes to decisions on real risks instead of extracting status
- The commit the VP hands upward is built on evidence they can defend

**The Outcome:** Forecast accuracy improves and pipeline reviews shrink from interrogations to decisions.

---

### Basiq Solution to Hurdle 5: New reps ramp inside the motion

**How Basiq Helps:** A new rep's day one is a served queue carrying the actual playbook, with full account and conversation history behind every inherited deal. The motion transfers through the platform instead of through shadowing whoever has time.

**Specific Benefits:**
- Ramp happens inside the real motion, not a stale PDF
- Inherited accounts come with complete context, so no relationship restarts
- Cohort execution is visible early, so a struggling hire surfaces in weeks, not quarters
- A bad hire and a bad handoff become distinguishable

**The Outcome:** Ramp compresses, and the never-productive hire rate stops being a silent tax on the plan.

---

### Basiq Solution to Hurdle 6: Execution discipline served, not remembered

**How Basiq Helps:** Follow-ups, recaps, multi-threading, and aging-deal touches arrive as prioritized tasks generated from conversation context. The steps that kill deals when skipped stop depending on rep memory.

**Specific Benefits:**
- Follow-up failure stops being a loss category
- Stalling deals surface as served tasks while intervention still works
- Multi-threading happens because it's the next task, not a best practice
- Momentum survives the rep's busiest weeks

**The Outcome:** Active pipeline converts at the rate the deals deserve.

## Why This Persona Cares About Basiq

The VP of Sales answers for the most scrutinized number in the company on the shortest clock in the building. Above them, the CRO and CEO want a commit they can trust and a plan that doesn't wobble. Below them, a team where three people carry the quarter, the middle improvises, managers have no coaching bandwidth, and every deal's reality lives in reps' heads until it's too late to act.

**The core dilemma: how do I hit this quarter's number with the team I have, when half my reps miss, the playbook runs a third of the time, coaching doesn't scale, and I can't see execution until it shows up in the miss?** Every tool offered so far records what happened or sequences what's next for one slice. None of them change what the whole team actually does all day.

**The emotional trigger** is the tenure clock. At 17–19 months average in seat, the VP doesn't get a rebuilding year. A quarter spent diagnosing is a quarter spent losing, and the miss lands on them personally before any structural explanation gets heard.

**The buying motivation** is a team that executes by default. Every rep's day becomes the playbook served in priority order with full context behind each task, selling capacity comes back without headcount, coaching lands on the exact skipped step, and the forecast is built on evidence. The VP gets the thing that earns the next seat: attainment produced by a system, visible to everyone above them.

## Differentiation Notes (vs. adjacent Basiq personas)

**vs. CRO:** The CRO owns all three revenue streams across quarters and buys lifecycle predictability: handoffs, NRR, board forecasts. The VP Sales owns new-business quota this quarter and buys team execution: attainment spread, coaching precision, ramp, selling time. The CRO asks "will the streams hit plan," the VP asks "who's off the play and what do I do about it this week." Never send lifecycle and seam messaging down to the VP; never send rep-level coaching messaging up to the CRO.

**vs. Founder/CEO:** The CEO buys the vision and the de-risked capital bet, with verification as the payoff. The VP Sales lives inside the motion daily and buys the operational reality: a team that executes by default. CEO messaging carries where GTM is going; VP messaging carries what Monday morning looks like.

**vs. VP/Director RevOps:** RevOps buys and operates the mechanism: capture, the approval gate, spend governance. The VP Sales consumes what the mechanism produces: execution visibility, clean pipeline data, a coachable team. The VP is often the loudest internal advocate once reps adopt, because rep adoption is the VP's proof. Send mechanism messaging to RevOps, outcome-at-team-level messaging to the VP.

**Use this persona for:** VP Sales outbound, quota-attainment and coaching content, ramp and rep-productivity content, pipeline-review and forecast-hygiene content, sales motions where the VP Sales is the champion or a co-signer.

**Do not use this persona for:** consulting-offer outreach (the consulting VP Sales persona covers that), lifecycle or NRR content (CRO), instrumentation and governance content (RevOps), or SDR-manager-level tactical content.
