# Founder/CEO Sequence Brief (Basiq Platform)

**Who:** CEO or founder-CEO, 10–5,000 employees, B2B with multi-meeting sales cycles. Signs personally at the smaller end; executive sponsor where a CRO owns the revenue org.
**Reserved word: Proof.** This card is about proving the motion is real, not about predictability (CRO's frame) or quota (VP Sales' frame). If a message drifts toward forecast accuracy or rep performance, it's on the wrong card.

---

## Triggers

### Funding and capital events

**Raised a Series A, B, or C in the last 90 days.**
Fresh capital means a scaling plan, and the scaling plan assumes the motion works when run. Nobody has verified that.
→ *Pain 4 (the blind bet), Pain 1 (can't verify execution)*

**PE acquisition or majority recap announced.**
New owners grade efficiency math from day one, and the CEO inherits the burden of decomposing it.
→ *Pain 4, Pain 3 (data nobody believes)*

### Hiring signals

**Multiple open AE/SDR roles at once.**
Headcount is being added to a playbook that has roughly 30% adoption today. Every hire is an experiment they're paying for.
→ *Pain 2 (playbook doesn't get run), Pain 4*

**First VP Sales or Head of Sales posting, or a new one announced.**
The founder's motion has to transfer, and 70% of first VP Sales hires fail. The new leader inherits either a running motion or a blank CRM.
→ *Pain 6 (motion can't leave heads)*

**VP Sales departure inside 24 months.**
The tenure math (17–19 month average) just played out at their company. The playbook likely left with the person.
→ *Pain 6, Pain 1*

**RevOps or Sales Ops job posting.**
They're hiring someone to fix data and process visibility. That's an admission the CRM rollups aren't trusted.
→ *Pain 3*

### Stated concerns

**CEO posts on LinkedIn about AI strategy, GTM efficiency, forecast surprises, or scaling pains.**
Strongest personalization available: quote their own framing back and extend it. A CEO writing publicly about the board's AI question is pre-qualifying themselves.
→ *Pain 1, Pain 4, or Pain 5 depending on the post*

**Earnings commentary or investor letters citing sales productivity, CAC, or efficiency (public or PE-owned).**
The efficiency story is being graded in public. They can report the number but can't decompose it.
→ *Pain 4*

### Product and market moves

**Repositioning, rebrand, or major product launch.**
New messaging just shipped to a team whose last messaging decayed from the day it was written. The loop that keeps it current doesn't exist.
→ *Pain 5 (buyer voice never comes back)*

**Growth stall visible in hiring freezes or flat headcount against stated growth targets.**
The quarter missed and the org is in diagnosis mode. Right now the diagnosis is a debate.
→ *Pain 1, Pain 3*

## Pain Points

**The wedge. If a message lands only one, land this one.**

### 1. When the number misses, nobody can tell why

The pipeline review turns into a debate: sellers, message, market, or the playbook itself. There's no evidence, so the org coaches reps for playbook problems and rewrites playbooks for skill problems. Two quarters burn finding out.
**Say it as:** "When your team misses a number, can you tell whether the playbook was actually run?"

### 2. The playbook has ~30% adoption, and not because reps are lazy

The rep's day starts with deciding what to do next across five tools, reconstructing context from memory before every action. The playbook is in a doc; the decision is in the deal. Friction wins.
**Say it as:** "Your real sales process is however many reps you have, that many private improvisations."
**Numbers:** ~30% playbook adoption (Chief); reps follow the designed process ~36% of the time (State of Sales Enablement); embedded-process reps hit quota at 3.7x (Gartner CSO via Supered).

### 3. Pipeline reviews run on data nobody in the room believes

Stages stale, close dates rolling forward 30 days on repeat, notes that read "discovery call." Enforcement produces compliance without quality.
**Say it as:** "Everyone in your pipeline review knows the CRM is fiction. The meeting continues anyway."
**Numbers:** 24% of CRM admins report less than half their data is accurate and complete (Validity via Databar).

### 4. Every GTM dollar is a blind bet the board grades later

Hiring plans and outbound budgets get approved against a motion nobody verified, and the efficiency math reports the truth months after the money moved.
**Say it as:** "You're scaling a motion you can't verify, on math your board grades dollar for dollar."
**Numbers:** Burn multiple called critical by 56% of seed and 83% of Series C+ investors (SaaS Capital); median SaaS spends $2.00 per $1 of new ARR (SaaS Mag).
**Use with care:** implies their current bets are blind. Works from a peer frame ("every CEO is making this bet"), lands badly as an accusation.

### 5. What buyers say never makes it back into what the company says

Hundreds of buyer conversations a month, none of it flowing into personas, value props, or site copy. The founder used to close this loop personally. They aren't in the deals anymore.
**Say it as:** "Your messaging was written at an offsite. Your buyers have said better since."

### 6. The best sellers' motion retires with them

Founder context, top-rep methods, none of it documented beyond a fraction. New hires improvise and close less, and nobody can tell if the hire was bad or the handoff was.
**Say it as:** "70% of first VP Sales hires fail. Most inherit a blank CRM and a 47-page PDF."
**Use with care:** strongest with founder-CEOs pre- or mid-transition. At 1,000+ employees, reframe to star-rep dispersion instead of founder transition.

## Proof Points

Basiq's proof reality: one paying customer, a small trial cohort, positioning unvalidated. The sequence below is built for that reality. The CEO's doubt is "is this real or another dashboard," so the mechanism leads and traction is never volunteered.

**1. The live mechanism.** A real transcript goes in; the task queue, CRM updates, and proposed doc edits come out, watchable in minutes. Leads because it's the one thing that can't be faked and directly answers "is this vaporware." Demo-first, always.

**2. The adoption design argument.** Every mandate-based tool produced incomplete data, which is why the CEO doesn't trust their CRM today. Basiq's seller surface leads, so reps use it voluntarily and completeness is the exhaust. Second because it explains why this time is different, which is the CEO's next question after the demo.

**3. The structural research.** 30% adoption, 36% process-following, 3.7x quota gap, 24% CRM data admission. Third because it reframes the pain as industry-structural, not their failure. Opens the conversation without indicting the room.

**4. The architecture honesty.** Salesforce or HubSpot stays. Gong can stay. No rip-and-replace, no IT war on day one. Fourth because it de-risks rather than persuades; use it when hesitation appears, not before.

**5. Operator credibility.** Built by founders who run revenue motions for client organizations, not by a product team guessing at sales work. Hold for "who are you people."

**Sequencing:** mechanism first, design argument second, research third. Architecture and operator credibility are held responses, not pitch material.

**The honest gap:** nothing yet proves outcomes. No win-rate lift, no efficiency improvement, no logo list, no customer quote worth using. Do not imply customers at scale or measured results. The closest defensible differentiation claim: research found no product doing the approve-or-decline document loop, so "nobody else does this" is usable where "it worked for X" is not. If asked directly about traction, say early and say it plainly. A CEO respects honest-early far more than caught-inflating.

## Notes

- **This card drops the buyer/sponsor split.** At the smaller end this person signs. Where a CRO exists, they sponsor and the CRO signs, and messaging built from this card should open a door, not close a deal. Check the account before choosing the CTA.
- **Inherited tension:** the entire positioning is a hypothesis per the source doc. Every claim on this card is a design argument or third-party research, not customer evidence. That's survivable in outbound only if the proof sequencing above is respected.
