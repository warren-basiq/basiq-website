# VP of Sales Sequence Brief (Basiq Platform)

**Who:** VP or Head of Sales, 100–5,000 employees, B2B with multi-meeting cycles, 10–150 quota-carrying reps. Signs where sales tooling is their line; co-signs and champions where the CRO owns the platform decision.
**Reserved word: Attainment.** This card is about spreading attainment across the whole team this quarter. Not proof the motion is real (CEO's frame), not predictability across streams (CRO's frame), not control of the machine (RevOps' frame). If a message drifts toward board narrative, NRR, or capture mechanics, it's on the wrong card.

---

## Triggers

### Role signals

**New VP Sales in seat within the last 90 days.**
New sales leaders audit the team, the pipeline, and the stack in their first quarter, and inherit either a running motion or a blank CRM. At 17–19 months average tenure, they need wins fast and know it.
→ *Pain 1 (top-heavy attainment), Pain 4 (pipeline reviews on fiction)*

**VP Sales role posted.**
The last one just left, likely inside two years, and the playbook may have left with them. Whoever lands inherits the diagnosis problem on day one. Time the outreach for their arrival.
→ *Pain 1, Pain 5 (ramp)*

**Frontline sales manager postings, or a "sales enablement manager" hire.**
Manager capacity is being added to a coaching problem that's structural, not headcount-shaped. Three hours per rep per month doesn't scale by hiring one more manager.
→ *Pain 3 (coaching doesn't scale)*

### Team and hiring signals

**Multiple open AE roles at once.**
Every hire is 5–6 months of ramp cost, and 27% of new B2B reps never hit quota. A hiring wave onto an improvised motion is the most expensive experiment the VP will run this year.
→ *Pain 5, Pain 1*

**Visible rep churn on LinkedIn (several departures from the same sales team in a quarter).**
Middle-of-the-curve reps leave when they're missing quota without coaching. The team that remains is more top-heavy than before.
→ *Pain 3, Pain 1*

**AE job postings that mention self-sourcing or "full-cycle" selling.**
The self-sourcing mandate just became an unfunded tax on a selling window that's already 40% of the day.
→ *Pain 2 (non-selling time)*

### Performance signals

**Missed quarter or lowered guidance (public or reported).**
The diagnosis debate is happening right now, and the VP is the person it lands on. They need "which 30% of the playbook ran" answered before the next QBR.
→ *Pain 4, Pain 6 (deals die from skipped steps)*

**Flat sales headcount against stated growth targets.**
The bigger number has to come from the same team, which means the 3.7x embedded-process gap is the only lever left.
→ *Pain 2, Pain 1*

### Stated concerns

**VP posts on LinkedIn about coaching, rep productivity, pipeline hygiene, or forecast pain.**
Strongest personalization available: quote their framing back and extend it. A VP posting about pipeline review theater is describing Pain 4 in their own words.
→ *Pain 3 or Pain 4 depending on the post*

### Cadence events

**Sales kickoff season (Dec–Feb) or a methodology rollout (MEDDIC, Challenger, etc.).**
A playbook investment is about to ship as a doc into a team that follows docs 36% of the time. Reach them while the rollout plan is still being written.
→ *Pain 1, Pain 6*

**Quarter-end weeks (last three weeks of March, June, September, December).**
The scramble is live: interrogation-style pipeline reviews, slipping commits, follow-up failures visible in real time. Reference the season, not their numbers.
→ *Pain 4, Pain 6*

## Pain Points

**The wedge. If a message lands only one, land this one.**

### 1. The number is carried by three reps, and the middle can't be fixed one rep at a time

Average attainment sits near 47%, top performers carry the plan, and the middle sixty percent runs private improvisations of the sales process. What the best reps do differently is invisible, so it can't be transferred, and one A-player departure breaks the quarter.
**Say it as:** "Your number is carried by three reps. What happens when one of them has a bad quarter?"
**Numbers:** ~47% average B2B quota attainment (Forrester); only 15% of teams have half their reps at 80%+ of quota (Ebsta x Pavilion); embedded-process reps hit quota at 3.7x (Gartner CSO via Supered).

### 2. The team sells at half its headcount

Reps spend 60% of their time on non-selling work: research they'll forget, CRM updates that are stale on arrival, deciding what to do next across five tools. A 20-rep team performs like 10, and the VP is asked to hit a bigger number with it anyway.
**Say it as:** "You're paying for 20 reps and getting 10. That's the headcount conversation nobody's having."
**Numbers:** 60% of rep time is non-selling (Salesforce State of Sales); 50% of sellers overwhelmed by their platform count (Gartner).

### 3. Coaching goes to whoever is already on fire

Meaningful coaching takes ~3 hours per rep per month and managers spend 30–60% of their time on admin. So coaching is reactive: the dead deal, the struggling rep. The middle of the curve, where coaching pays best, gets nothing, and the top-to-average gap widens every quarter.
**Say it as:** "Your managers coach whoever's on fire. The middle of your team, where the money is, gets nothing."
**Numbers:** Only 40% of reps get weekly coaching, the frequency that moves attainment; 75% of reps say they'd hit target with a coach (MySalesCoach; Salesforce).

### 4. Pipeline reviews are interrogations because the data is fiction

Stages stale, close dates rolling 30 days on repeat, notes that read "good call." The VP extracts deal reality verbally, one deal at a time, then hands up a commit they privately discount.
**Say it as:** "Your pipeline review is a cross-examination because the CRM is fiction and everyone knows it."
**Numbers:** 24% of CRM admins report less than half their data is accurate and complete (Validity via Databar); ~44% of deals slip.

### 5. Ramp takes half a year and a quarter of hires never get there

New reps inherit a stale 47-page playbook, accounts with no usable history, and a motion they reconstruct by shadowing. Every hire is two quarters of cost before production, on the VP's 17–19 month clock.
**Say it as:** "Your new AE won't produce for six months, and there's a one-in-four chance they never do."
**Numbers:** 5.3-month average AE ramp, median 6, longer above $50K ACV (Bridge Group); ~27% of new B2B reps never hit quota (Gartner).
**Use with care:** lands hardest right after a hiring announcement; avoid implying their recent hires were mistakes.

### 6. Deals die from skipped steps, not lost arguments

The follow-up that never went out, the champion never multi-threaded, the recap that would have kept momentum. Visible in every post-mortem, invisible in time to act. Loss reasons say "price" and "timing" when the honest answer is "we stopped working it."
**Say it as:** "Your losses say 'price' and 'timing.' The real reason is the follow-up that never went out."

## Proof Points

The VP's doubt is speed and rep reception: "will this produce inside my clock, and will my reps actually use it or revolt?" The sequence answers reception first because everything else depends on it, and never promises outcome timelines that don't exist yet. Traction is never volunteered.

**1. The live mechanism, shown as the rep's morning.** A real transcript goes in; the next day's queue comes out: prep, post-game, follow-ups, multi-threading, in priority order. Leads because the VP evaluates every tool through one lens, "what does my reps' Monday look like," and this demo answers it in minutes.

**2. The adoption design argument.** Reps resent every tool bought for leadership, and the VP has watched sequencers and CI tools become shelfware. Basiq's seller surface leads: the queue makes the rep's day easier, so adoption is structural. Second because rep revolt is the VP's most expensive failure mode, and this answers it.

**3. The coaching visibility payoff.** Task completion per rep, per play, plus MEDDIC movement per deal from post-game output. Managers coach the specific skipped step without spending hours finding it. Third because it's the VP's own leverage upgrade: same managers, precision coaching.

**4. The structural research.** 47% attainment, 3.7x embedded-process gap, 60% non-selling time, coaching frequency data. Fourth because it frames the dispersion problem as structural rather than the VP's leadership failure, which keeps the conversation open.

**5. The architecture honesty.** Salesforce or HubSpot stays, Gong can stay, and rollout doesn't require a data migration or an IT project. Held for hesitation; the VP's version of this concern is "I don't have a quarter to spend on implementation."

**6. Operator credibility.** Built by founders who run revenue motions for client organizations and carry quotas of their own. Hold for "who are you people."

**Sequencing:** rep's-morning demo first, adoption design second, coaching visibility third. Research, architecture, and credibility are held responses.

**The honest gap:** nothing yet proves attainment lift, ramp compression, or forecast improvement anywhere, and no VP can call a peer reference. Do not promise results inside a quarter; the defensible claim is what the demo shows plus the design argument for why reps adopt it. The one uniqueness claim that holds: research found no product doing the approve-or-decline document loop. If asked about traction, say early and plainly. This persona lives on a short clock and will respect directness far more than a dodge.

## Notes

- **This card runs a champion-or-co-signer motion by default.** The VP signs only where sales tooling is their budget line; at platform scale the CRO signs and the VP champions. Rep adoption is the VP's proof, which makes them the loudest internal advocate once a pilot runs. Pick the CTA accordingly: pilot-shaped for the VP, platform-shaped for the CRO.
- **Watch the altitude line with the CRO card.** Quota, coaching, and ramp stay here; NRR, handoffs, and board forecasts stay on the CRO card. Cross-contamination is easiest between these two.
- **Inherited tension:** positioning is a hypothesis per the source doc. Every claim here is a design argument or third-party research, not customer evidence.
